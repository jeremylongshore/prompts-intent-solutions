## Core Review Framework: The COMPASS Method

### C - Connect & Context Setting
- **Purpose:** Establish psychological safety and review objectives
- **Duration:** 5-10 minutes
- **Key Actions:**
  - Acknowledge employee contributions since last review
  - Clarify review purpose and process
  - Set collaborative tone for discussion

### O - Observe & Acknowledge Achievements
- **Purpose:** Recognize accomplishments and strengths
- **Duration:** 10-15 minutes
- **Key Actions:**
  - Highlight specific achievements with measurable impact
  - Identify demonstrated strengths and growth areas
  - Connect individual wins to team/company goals

### M - Measure Performance Against Goals
- **Purpose:** Objective assessment of goal completion
- **Duration:** 15-20 minutes
- **Key Actions:**
  - Review each goal with specific metrics
  - Analyze factors contributing to success/challenges
  - Document lessons learned and best practices

### P - Plan Development Opportunities
- **Purpose:** Identify growth areas and skill development needs
- **Duration:** 10-15 minutes
- **Key Actions:**
  - Discuss career aspirations and interests
  - Identify skill gaps and development opportunities
  - Explore stretch assignments and learning resources

### A - Align Future Goals & Expectations
- **Purpose:** Set clear, achievable goals for next period
- **Duration:** 15-20 minutes
- **Key Actions:**
  - Collaborate on SMART goals aligned with business objectives
  - Define success metrics and checkpoints
  - Establish support needed from manager/organization

### S - Summarize & Schedule Follow-ups
- **Purpose:** Ensure clarity and commitment to action items
- **Duration:** 5-10 minutes
- **Key Actions:**
  - Recap key discussion points and agreements
  - Schedule regular check-ins and development activities
  - Confirm mutual understanding of next steps

## Difficult Conversation Frameworks

### Framework 1: The SBI-I Model (Situation-Behavior-Impact-Intent)

**Structure:**
1. **Situation:** Describe the specific situation/context
2. **Behavior:** State the observable behavior (facts only)
3. **Impact:** Explain the impact on you/team/business
4. **Intent:** Ask about their intent/perspective

**Example:**
"During yesterday's client meeting (Situation), I noticed you interrupted the client three times during their presentation (Behavior). This seemed to frustrate them, and they became less engaged in our discussion (Impact). Can you help me understand what was going through your mind during that interaction? (Intent)"

### Framework 2: The DESC Script (Describe-Express-Specify-Consequences)

**Structure:**
1. **Describe:** State the facts objectively
2. **Express:** Share your feelings/thoughts
3. **Specify:** Request specific changes
4. **Consequences:** Explain positive outcomes of change

**Example:**
"I've noticed that project deadlines have been missed three times in the past month (Describe). I'm concerned about our team's ability to deliver on commitments to clients (Express). Going forward, I need you to provide weekly status updates and flag any potential delays 48 hours in advance (Specify). This will help us maintain client trust and support you in managing workload effectively (Consequences)."

### Framework 3: The COIN Method (Context-Observation-Impact-Next)

**Structure:**
1. **Context:** Set the stage for the conversation
2. **Observation:** Share specific observations
3. **Impact:** Discuss the broader implications
4. **Next:** Collaborate on solutions

**Example:**
"I'd like to discuss your participation in team meetings (Context). Over the past month, I've observed that you've been quiet during discussions and haven't shared your ideas (Observation). Your expertise is valuable, and the team loses the benefit of your insights when you don't contribute (Impact). What can we do together to help you feel more comfortable sharing your thoughts? (Next)"

## Feedback Delivery Best Practices

### The 4:1 Positive-to-Constructive Ratio
- **Research Foundation:** High-performing teams receive 4 positive interactions for every 1 constructive feedback
- **Implementation:** Start with genuine appreciation, then address improvement areas
- **Example Structure:**
  1. Appreciate specific recent contribution
  2. Acknowledge growth/effort in development area
  3. Recognize collaboration/attitude
  4. Highlight unique strength/value-add
  5. Address constructive feedback item

### The GROW Coaching Model
**Goal:** What does the employee want to achieve?
**Reality:** What's the current situation?
**Options:** What options are available?
**Will/Way Forward:** What will they do?

### Active Listening Techniques
- **Paraphrasing:** "What I'm hearing is..."
- **Clarifying:** "Can you help me understand..."
- **Summarizing:** "So the key points are..."
- **Empathizing:** "That sounds challenging..."

## Goal Setting & Performance Metrics

### SMART-ER Goals Framework
- **S**pecific: Clear, well-defined outcomes
- **M**easurable: Quantifiable success metrics
- **A**chievable: Realistic given resources/timeline
- **R**elevant: Aligned with role and business objectives
- **T**ime-bound: Specific deadlines and milestones
- **E**valuated: Regular progress check-ins
- **R**eadjusted: Flexible based on changing priorities

### Performance Metrics by Role Type

**Sales Roles:**
- Revenue targets and pipeline metrics
- Customer acquisition and retention rates
- Activity metrics (calls, meetings, proposals)
- Customer satisfaction scores

**Technical Roles:**
- Project delivery timelines and quality metrics
- Code quality and technical debt reduction
- Knowledge sharing and documentation
- Innovation and process improvement initiatives

**Management Roles:**
- Team performance and development metrics
- Employee engagement and retention rates
- Process efficiency and cost management
- Cross-functional collaboration effectiveness

**Customer Service Roles:**
- Response time and resolution rates
- Customer satisfaction and NPS scores
- Case volume and complexity handling
- Knowledge base contributions

## Documentation & Legal Compliance

### Essential Documentation Elements
1. **Objective Performance Data:** Quantifiable metrics and results
2. **Specific Behavioral Examples:** Date, time, witnesses, impact
3. **Support Provided:** Training, resources, coaching given
4. **Employee Response:** Acknowledgment, agreement, or disagreement
5. **Action Plans:** Specific steps and timelines
6. **Follow-up Schedule:** Regular check-in dates

### Legal Protection Best Practices
- Use factual, observable language (avoid subjective opinions)
- Document patterns of behavior over time
- Include witness statements when appropriate
- Maintain consistency across similar situations
- Follow company policy and legal requirements
- Involve HR in performance improvement plans

## Performance Improvement Strategies

### For Skill Gaps
1. **Assessment:** Use skill matrix to identify specific gaps
2. **Learning Plan:** Combine formal training with on-job practice
3. **Mentoring:** Pair with skilled colleague for guidance
4. **Practice Projects:** Assign low-risk opportunities to build competency
5. **Regular Feedback:** Weekly coaching sessions with specific skill focus

### For Motivation Issues
1. **Root Cause Analysis:** Understand underlying causes
2. **Role Alignment:** Assess fit between role and interests/strengths
3. **Career Pathing:** Connect current role to future aspirations
4. **Recognition:** Implement appropriate appreciation strategies
5. **Autonomy:** Increase decision-making authority where appropriate

### For Behavioral Concerns
1. **Clear Expectations:** Document specific behavioral requirements
2. **Immediate Feedback:** Address issues promptly and directly
3. **Coaching Support:** Provide tools and strategies for improvement
4. **Peer Support:** Facilitate positive team relationships
5. **Accountability:** Regular check-ins with consequences for non-compliance
